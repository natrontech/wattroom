How Ride Worlds plays

Seven screens for M11, drawn from the decisions Jan made on 26 September 2026 and the eight research threads behind them. The route in every screen is the Gurnigel loop: 51.7 km and 1,312 m of climbing from Stollmatt over the Gurnigelpass, with OpenStreetMap roads and swisstopo heights. Numbers come from that route, the climb detector and the ERG simulation unless a note says otherwise.

Watt magenta is a live number: your watts, and your dot on a profile. It is the only thing that glows.

Neon violet is structure: panels, the route line, selected tabs. It never glows.

Zones 1 to 7 are effort: the ring under a rider, the trace you rode, workout blocks.

Gradient uses five violet steps, lighter when steeper: under 3, 3 to 6, 6 to 9, 9 to 12, over 12 %. Zone colours stay free for effort.

Try it:The data-page tabs in the bike computer switch too.
  • Panels are flat. Surface colour at 86 % opacity with a hairline border, no blur and no shadow over the canvas, so the GPU budget from #2998 holds. Watt magenta appears once: your 3 s power and your dot.
  • Data pages change only the bike computer. Ride, Power and Map swap its fields. The road line, the climb card and the bunch never page away, which is where Garmin loses riders who page off the workout. Handlebar remotes send PgUp and PgDn, which never collide with push-to-talk or soundboard keys.
  • The climb card opens by itself 400 m before any climb of class IV or harder. Classes use Garmin's current score cut-offs, length × average grade: IV above 8,000, III above 16,000, II above 32,000, I above 64,000, HC above 80,000. The Gurnigelpass scores 78,960, class I, just under HC. Roman numerals keep a climb class from reading like a rider Category.
  • Names over riders show only for the two nearest riders and anyone speaking. Mia is talking, so her label carries the speaking ring. Offsets in the bunch list stay within 10 m behind while a rider pedals. Nobody is ranked in a bunch ride.
  • The live zone sits on the ring under your wheels, not on the jersey. That frees the jersey for the shop and amends design-final decision 15.

Sweet Spot 2×20 with six riders, 54:40 in, twenty seconds before the second block ends. Each trainer holds its own rider's target in ERG. The bunch moves at the prescribed block's pace for a reference rider (225 W × 90 % on the real grade), so it goes exactly where the plan says.   • The next block is painted on the road, 60 m ahead, with its target, and chevrons lead to it. The pace follows the plan, so the paint lands exactly where the block starts. A bunch paced by the crew's live mean drifted about 1.5 % from the plan in the simulation, roughly 450 m over an hour.
  • Nobody is dropped. Each rider's offset is a decaying measure of effort against their own target, kept between 10 m behind and 25 m ahead while pedalling. Tom rides at a 0.95 bias and is not punished for it. A rider who stops pulls over and the team car tows them back.
  • The chip "ERG: the road is scenery" tells riders the trainer ignores the grade. 259 W on a 6.9 % ramp would otherwise read as a broken trainer.
  • Terrain Match on the real loop. From km 0 the 65-minute workout covers 13.5 km and spends 30 minutes under 10 km/h. From km 29.3 it covers 31.2 km and 407 m with 5 minutes under 10 km/h: the first block rides the Schwarzwasser gorge and the drag to Riggisberg, the recovery drops into Stollmatt, the second block climbs.
  • The Skyline carries the workout band under the road, so a glance shows which block lands on which climb.

  • Real names from OSM. v1 used generated names such as Selibpass. With coordinates kept on the server, the pass sign says Gurnigelpass and the climb card names come from OSM: a pass or saddle within 150 m of the top first, then a peak, then the road's name.
  • km 0 is a fixed public place. The first public anchor outside every privacy zone, here the Dorfbrunnen, a short ride from the start. It stays the same for every route from home, so importing many routes leaks nothing new. Random end trims would leak: people found hidden homes up to 68 % of the time from a few routes (CHI 2022).
  • No third-party map host. The map comes from our own weekly OSM extract (Geofabrik Switzerland, 521 MB) served from WattRoom's origin, so no viewer's position goes to a tile company. Credit sits in the corner.
  • How comes before where it starts. Ride it together, workout on it, race it. A workout-first pick gets Terrain Match, which found the Sweet Spot start at km 29.3 on this loop.
  • Famous climbs stay a small fixed set, like the workout library, so they never become a content treadmill (WATTROOM.md:31).

Desk layout at 1180 px. On a narrow screen it shrinks, then scrolls sideways inside the frame.

  • Your time is yours only when your own watts moved your dot: a route ride, a solo road step, a race. In a bunch ride everyone shares one position, so a climb time belongs to the crew and becomes a crew record. In ERG the trainer set the watts, so the time measures the workout.
  • VAM shows only on climbs averaging 5 % or more that take 3 minutes or longer. Schwefelberg at 3.3 % gets none. Garmin's climb score is exactly 100 × net gain, so the classes also read as metres: IV above 80 m, III above 160 m, I above 640 m, HC above 800 m.
  • The crew climb board is off until an owner turns it on, each rider opts in, it resets weekly and it is bracketed by Category. That keeps ADR-0036's weekly board and needs one amendment for the new column.
  • kJ go into the wallet 1:1, inside the ride's own save. Deleting the ride keeps them, as ADR-0047 does for XP. Spending never touches XP or level.
  • A shared ride carries no map by default. Friends get numbers and the climb silhouette, never the trace.

Desk layout at 1180 px. On a narrow screen it shrinks, then scrolls sideways inside the frame.

  • Prices read as riding. Anchored on SPEC's typical 45-minute ride of about 600 kJ: colours 600 (a ride), kits and parts 1,800 (a week), frames and wheels 7,200 (a month), the Ordonnanzvelo 21,600 (a season). Each card can also say "about N of your rides", counted from your own last eight weeks.
  • No colour near the watt hue, no zone colours, nothing that glows. The catalogue check refuses a kit within 20° of magenta, so your trail stays the only glow and a jersey never reads as live data.
  • No random rewards, no daily-login bonus, no time-limited sales, no trading. Seasonal items such as Alpabzug are earned by riding in the window and come back every year. The terms admit riders under 16 with a parent, and EU policy is moving against reward loops for minors.
  • Buying is immediate with a 10-second undo, no confirm dialog. It runs under the rider's row lock, so two tabs can't spend the same kJ twice. A button that can't be afforded is disabled with how many rides it takes.
  • Skin tone and body build are picked by the rider, free, and never sold. Body build is never derived from weight, which would publish a sensitive number to every spectator.

  • Explorer tiles are OpenStreetMap zoom-14 tiles, about 1.7 km a side at this latitude. The Gurnigel loop lights 30 of them and its largest full square is 2×2. The map only draws what you rode, never the unridden rest of a route.
  • A pass is stamped when you cross within 50 m of a named OSM pass or saddle after 50 m or more of gain in the last 2 km, so rolling down to it gives no credit.
  • The Everest ladder counts virtual gain at any trainer difficulty, as Zwift does. "Everesting" is Hells500's brand with its own rules, so the ladder never uses the word.
  • No XP per tile or per kilometre. XP stays 1 kJ = 1 XP, so riders choose routes to train, not to farm.
  • This is the seed of the crew's valley (#3035): tiles the crew lit together could become the persistent crew world, built on real ground.
